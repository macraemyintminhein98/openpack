# Lesson self-audit (2026-11-25)

A second pass by the AI author over every lesson line against the listed sources. This is
**not** an expert review and does not replace one. It records what was checked and changed.

## Changed in this pass

| Lesson | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Safe cooking fires | Oil-fire tip rewritten: never water, slide a lid on, remove the heat, leave the lid on. Removed "or a wet cloth". | The fire-service sources agree on the lid and on never using water; the wet cloth was not in them. |
| Safe cooking fires, Burns | "Stop, drop and roll" tip now includes covering the face, and a US Fire Administration source was added. | The tip had no listed source. |

## Deliberately not included

| Topic | Reason |
|---|---|
| Home-made rehydration drink (sugar and salt recipe) | Sources disagree on amounts, and one first-aid body warns against adding kitchen salt to water. Lessons say to use ORS packets as labelled. |
| Bleach or chlorine drops for water | The right dose depends on the product strength. Lessons say to follow the packet. |
| Pressure bandage for snake bite | Recommended in some countries only, for some snakes. WHO's general advice is to immobilise and not use a tight tourniquet. Needs a local expert. |
| Tourniquet for bleeding | Needs training. |
| Adding a second cloth when blood soaks through | Guidance differs between organisations. The lesson says to keep pressing and not to keep lifting the cloth. |
| Edible and poisonous plants | Too dangerous to teach with simple drawings. |

## Points a reviewer should look at first

1. **Snake bite.** Is "splint and carry, no pressure bandage" right for Myanmar's snakes
   (Russell's viper, cobras, kraits)? Where is antivenom actually available?
2. **Heat illness.** Is "30 minutes without improvement" the right threshold to give?
3. **Burns.** "20 minutes of running water" assumes water is available. Is the fallback
   (pouring clean water, wet cloth) acceptable?
4. **Water.** The sun-and-bottle method is listed only as a tip. Should it be removed for
   the rainy season?
5. **Burmese medical words.** Especially ဓာတ်ဆားရည် (ORS), အပူရှပ်ခြင်း (heatstroke),
   အူရောင်ငန်းဖျား (typhoid), မြွေဆိပ်ဖြေဆေး (antivenom).
