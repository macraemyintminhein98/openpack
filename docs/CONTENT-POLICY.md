# Content policy (DRAFT)

> Draft written without legal advice. Have a lawyer who knows the law where you operate
> review it before you distribute packs. Nothing here is legal advice.

## What goes in a pack

1. **Only content we are allowed to share.** Every item names its licence, its author and
   where it came from. The builder refuses items without an allowed licence
   (`lib/licenses.js`). "Found on the internet" is not a licence.
2. **No pirated films, music, TV, books, games or paid apps.** Not even "just for testing".
3. **Apps (APK) only from the developer's own release**, with the published checksum pinned,
   and with a source-code link for copyleft apps.
4. **Useful, non-partisan material first:** health, education, farming, reference,
   children's reading, practical skills, open-source apps.
5. **No content that puts carriers at risk.** A person carrying a USB stick should never be
   endangered by what is on it. When unsure, leave it out.
6. **No personal data.** Packs contain no accounts, trackers or logs, and the server keeps
   no record of who read what.
7. **No hate speech, incitement to violence, sexual content, scams or medical claims
   without a credible source.**

## Who decides

The pack maintainer who holds the signing key decides what is in a signed pack and is
responsible for it. Anyone may build their own pack with their own key; it is then theirs.

## Reporting a problem or asking for removal

Open an issue or write to the maintainer (contact in README). Say which pack version and
which item, and why (your rights are affected, the licence is wrong, it is unsafe).

- We acknowledge within 7 days.
- If the claim is plausible, the item is removed from the next pack while it is checked.
- The change note of that release lists the removal.
- Packs already copied cannot be recalled. This is a limit of offline distribution, and one
  more reason to be strict before publishing.

## Licences

Code: MIT. Each content item keeps its own licence. Items marked non-commercial must not be
sold, and nobody may charge for the content itself (a charge for the blank media or the
copying service is a separate matter: ask a lawyer).
